Betting 101

3-minute read

Odds = "out of 100"

"Florida wins 58 of 100" means: play this game 100 times and Florida wins about 58, loses about 42. That's not a lock. 42 of 100 happens all the time. Our win chances are tested: when the model said 75, the team won 77 of 100 (2021–2025).

Reading the price

−110: risk $110 to win $100. +132: risk $100 to win $132. −156: risk $156 to win $100. Every price hides the win rate you need just to break even:

break-even (of 100) = 100 × risk ÷ (risk + win)

At −110 that's 52.4 of 100. Winning 51 of 100 still loses money.

Value and ROI

Value is what you make on average per $100 bet. Win 56 of 100 at −110: you collect 56 × $90.91 and lose 44 × $100, which is +$6.90 per $100. That's a +6.9% ROI (return on investment).

value = wins × payout − losses × $100

Risk

Even a real 55-of-100 bettor is down after 100 bets about 3 times in 10, just from luck. That's why pros bet small: 1 unit = 1% of your bankroll on a Best Bet, half a unit on a Lean, and they judge themselves over hundreds of bets, not one weekend.

What the model looks at

Track record (opponent-adjusted ratings from ~2 years, plus Elo), current form (last 3 games, playing above or below its level), efficiency (plays, success rate, explosive plays), roster (talent, returning starters) and context (home field, rest, likely game script).

How good is it?

It's reliable at who wins. Against sportsbook lines it hasn't proven an edge yet, which is why we're in practice mode. The one signal that has held up (unders in games that look like shootouts) is what shows up in Best Bets.
